### Brain introduction
- mutliple areas
- networks of neurons that creates electrical signal through chemical reactions. 
- how to mesure brain activity 
	- chemical activity vs electromagnetic 
	- invasive (IRM, casque infrarouge) vs non-invasive
- Comparaison of the different signals
	- spatial and temporal resolution (all techniques will have a value of this two parameters)
--> They chose EEG (non-invasive, portable, high temporal resolution, well known, also some cons)![[Pasted image 20260227132031.png]]

but why ? 
	- brain computer interface (what they do)
	- Neuroscience (epilepic seizure, ...)

goal : motor imagery (MI) BCI : the robot will do what the brain tells him. 

pipeline : Signal acquisition --> Pre-processing --> feature extraction --> Classification (ML) --> Perform request action 

they have continuous signal and they put event markers (when they asked the person to moove or think about smtg) (then they compare)
issues :  Noisy, More ML classes is harder to classify, user training necessary, hard to gather ML training data, lack of standardised evaluation for BCI prototypes. 

### their study : 
15 participant (14 male, 1 female-
aged between 18 and 50 years (mean 28+-7)
5 sessions each and no prior BCI experience
they needed to put conducting electric gel for all eletroide (takes a lot of time)
first 2 sessions are familiarization (no feedback, alternate executing the movement and imaginating it)
session 3-5 : data acquisition (first without feedback(calibration) and then 3+ feedbacks runs )

### how does this control system works 
shared control approach (semi autonomous robot, user only needs to share high level commands)
goal : enable complex interactions while keeping the ML decoding simple 
key components : 
	- augmented reality user interface 
	- spacial awareness and object detection 
	- object selection with eye tracking 
	- action selection with MI BCI (2 classes for this concept)
